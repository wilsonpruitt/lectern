# Call to Worship — voice spec

Distilled from Wilson's own calls to worship written for Covenant UMC Austin
(20 examples with text, pulled from Circuit worship planning, 2026-06-30 — see
`circuit-corpus.json`). This is the target voice for Lectern's call-to-worship
drafts. It is descriptive of what Wilson already writes, not an imposed style.

## Form

- **Antiphonal / responsive.** Leader speaks a plain line; the congregation
  answers. In the corpus the congregation's part is marked **bold**; keep that
  convention (leader = plain, response = bold).
- **2 to 4 exchanges — 4 to 8 lines total.** That is the range; stay inside it.
  Four couplets (8 lines) is the long end, two (4 lines) the short. Never a
  monologue. Brevity is the discipline — if a couplet isn't pulling weight, cut
  it rather than round up.
- **Short lines.** One breath each. A leader line rarely runs past two clauses;
  responses are usually shorter than the prompt that draws them out.
- **A gathering turn at the end.** The last exchange resolves the movement —
  into "come, let us worship," a doxology, an Amen, or a return to the opening
  refrain. It lands; it does not trail off.

## Diction

- **Contemporary plain English.** No "thee/thou/dost." Direct address to God
  and to the gathered people. Warm, not formal.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "The way is long, and we are thirsty" — not "we
  acknowledge our spiritual need." Images (water, fire, breath, road, harvest,
  empty tomb, walls) carry the theology.
- **No throat-clearing.** No "We gather this morning to..." preambles. The first
  line is already the call.
- **Spare.** Cut every word that isn't pulling weight. Restraint is the register.

## Scriptural grounding (the "scripturally informed" part)

- The call is **woven from the day's texts**, not a quotation of them. Psalm 118
  becomes the Palm Sunday Hosanna call; John 14's "many dwelling places" becomes
  an Eastertide exchange; living-water imagery threads the Lent-A Samaritan-woman
  arc. The congregation should *recognize* the readings without being told the
  citation.
- **Lean on the Psalm and the Gospel** most heavily — they carry the affective
  and narrative weight. The first reading and epistle feed images.
- **Never proof-text.** No "as it says in...". The Scripture is the water the
  call swims in, not a slide cited on screen.

## Season is the spine

- A call belongs to its season first, its Sunday second.
- **Refrains carry the season** across consecutive weeks. The corpus shows two
  sustained ones:
  - Lent: *"Welcome pilgrims on the way to the cross. / **We are learning to
    follow Jesus.**"* opening, closing *"Pilgrims on the way, come let us worship
    God! / **We come to worship God as we learn to live inside out! Amen.**"*
  - Eastertide: *"Christ is risen! / **Christ is risen, Alleluia!**"* as the
    fixed response under varying leader lines.
- A series running several Sundays may take its own refrain. When Lectern drafts
  a run of Sundays, it should propose a refrain and reuse it, varying only the
  leader lines — the way the corpus does.

## What to avoid (AI-tells that would betray the voice)

- Triads and parallelism stacked for their own sake ("we gather, we pray, we
  hope").
- Adjective pile-ups ("gracious, loving, ever-present God").
- Greeting-card abstraction ("on this beautiful morning").
- Over-explaining the move. Trust the image.
- Em-dash-and-summarize cadence; vary the rhythm.
- Any citation, rubric, or stage direction inside the call text itself.

## Two corpus exemplars to hold in mind

**Lent (3rd Sunday, living water):**
> Welcome pilgrims on the way to the cross.
> **We are learning to follow Jesus.**
> The way is long, and we are thirsty.
> **We seek the living waters of Jesus.**
> ...
> Pilgrims on the way, come let us worship God!
> **We come to worship God as we learn to live inside out! Amen.**

**Pentecost (Come, Holy Spirit):**
> Come, Holy Spirit,
> **The fire of God, the breath of Life.**
> Come, Holy Spirit,
> **Our Advocate, our Counselor.**
> ...
> Come, Holy Spirit.
> **May we feel God's fire through our worship.**

## Note: greetings vs. calls

The corpus also holds a few **greetings** (kind: "greeting") — shorter, two-beat
exchanges drawn from the BCP/UMH pattern ("The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be
with you. / **And also with you.**"). These are a related but distinct form;
keep them tagged separately and don't blur the two.
