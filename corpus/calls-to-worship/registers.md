# Call to Worship — three registers

Lectern drafts **three stylistically distinct calls per Sunday**, all grounded in
the same day's readings (see `voice.md` for the shared rules: antiphonal,
Scripture woven not quoted, season-as-spine, a gathering turn). They differ in
*register* — the room they're written for — not in their text base.

The point is choice: same readings, three doors, pick the one that fits the
congregation and the day. They are mutually exclusive options, not a menu to mix.

---

## A — Spare (the house voice)

Wilson's own voice, fully specced in `voice.md`. **2–4 exchanges, 4–8 lines.**
Restraint is the register: short lines, concrete images, no padding, the
Scripture carried by allusion. This is the default and the approved baseline.

## B — Immersive

A sensory, thematic, building style — the expansive, pastoral register many
worship-design planners favor (we don't name the practitioner it evokes; those
who know the school will recognize it). *Wilson's note: not his taste, but many
planners prefer it — so it's a first-class option, written to its own standard,
not as a watered version of A.*

- **Built on one sensory anchor** for the day (smell the bread, feel the water,
  the warmth of the fire) — something the body can imagine, repeated.
- **A building congregational refrain.** One unison line the people say back
  several times, gaining force each round ("There is a place for you at this
  table"). Repetition is the engine; it's meant to land emotionally by the third
  return.
- **Warmer, more expansive, more pastoral.** Longer leader lines, direct
  invitation, casual welcome ("come as you are," "pull up a chair, friends").
  Names the people's actual states (tired, grieving, far off).
- **Not bound by the 4–8 line cap** — the immersive register breathes longer.
  Still antiphonal, still woven from the readings, still ends with a turn.
- Failure mode to avoid: sentimentality / greeting-card warmth untethered from
  the text. The sensory anchor must come *from the readings*, not decoration.

## C — Hybrid

The discipline of A with one move borrowed from B: **the house spareness plus a
single repeated refrain and one sensory anchor.** Short lines, no sprawl, but the
congregation gets a unison line that returns and a body-image to hold.

- Keep A's economy (stay near 4–8 lines) and concrete diction.
- Borrow B's **one** building refrain and **one** sensory anchor — no more.
- The bridge for a planner who finds A too austere and B too much.
